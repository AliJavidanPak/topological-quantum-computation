# Topological Quantum Computation

A theoretical study of topological quantum computation, focusing on anyonic systems, fusion rules, braiding, and their applications to quantum computation.

## Overview

This project studies the mathematical and physical foundations of topological quantum computation. It introduces anyonic systems and develops the formalism needed to describe their fusion and exchange properties, followed by an examination of how these properties can be used to encode and manipulate quantum information.

The project discusses:

- Abelian and non-Abelian anyons
- Fusion and splitting spaces
- Fusion rules and fusion matrices
- $F$-symbols and basis transformations
- The pentagon equation
- Braiding and $R$-matrices
- Fibonacci and Ising anyons
- Universality in the quantum circuit model
- Encoding qubits using anyons
- The Brylinski theorem and universal quantum computation
- The DiVincenzo criteria for quantum computation

## Anyon Models

Two important anyon theories are studied in detail:

### Fibonacci Anyons

The Fibonacci model is introduced through the fusion rule

$$
\tau \times \tau = I + \tau.
$$

Its $F$- and $R$-matrices are derived and used to construct braid-group representations. The role of Fibonacci anyons in universal topological quantum computation is then discussed.

### Ising Anyons

The Ising model is described by the particle types

$$
\{I,\sigma,\psi\},
$$

with

$$
\sigma \times \sigma = I + \psi.
$$

The corresponding $F$- and $R$-matrices are examined, together with the limitations of braiding Ising anyons for universal quantum computation.

## Contents

The accompanying report develops the subject in three stages:

1. Introduction to topological quantum computation and anyonic systems
2. Fusion rules, fusion spaces, $F$-symbols, and the pentagon equation
3. Braiding, $R$-matrices, and the use of anyons for quantum computation
